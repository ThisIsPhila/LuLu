# LuLu — Lean Business Canvas review

24 October 2026. Edited for owner review. Financial figures and pricing remain planning assumptions from the supplied discussion. No partnerships, adoption results or completed security capabilities are inferred.

## Problem Statement

Gender-based violence, harassment and abuse remain significant challenges in South Africa. The HSRC's First South African National Gender-Based Violence Study, 2022, published in 2024, found that 35.5% of women aged 18 and older had experienced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetime.

Beyond the violence itself, people face barriers to documenting incidents discreetly, preserving evidence, accessing appropriate support and deciding whether to disclose or report their experiences. Records may be fragmented across messages, photographs and personal accounts, while seeking assistance can mean revealing sensitive information before someone is ready. LuLu addresses the need for accessible, survivor-directed documentation and support that preserves the individual's control over their information.

## Solution & Features

LuLu is a discreet, mobile-first personal safety and documentation platform built around a useful everyday journal and storybook experience.

The planned protected experience combines concealed, passkey-mediated access with incident documentation, supporting photographs, messages, audio and documents, and an organised chronology. Contextual transformation changes identifying details and narrative perspective into coherent alternative accounts while preserving protected access to the author's original meaning. Ordinary journalling remains available without activating privacy processing.

The product direction includes evidence-integrity records, selective disclosure, discreet exit, cross-device continuity and recovery, and relevant support and referral pathways. Privacy-preserving insights into recurring patterns remain exploratory. Users choose whether, when and how to seek assistance or share information.

## Unique Value Proposition

Your experience. Your evidence. Your decision.

LuLu is designed to help people document sensitive experiences discreetly, preserve important information and explore support options without pressure to disclose or formally report.

By bringing an everyday journal together with protected access, contextual narrative concealment, evidence organisation and selective sharing, LuLu aims to preserve continuity and control from the first record to whatever step the individual chooses next.

## Unfair Advantage

LuLu's intended competitive advantage is the integration of discreet access, contextual narrative transformation, evidence continuity and user-controlled disclosure within an authentic everyday writing experience.

This combines capabilities that people would otherwise have to manage across separate journalling, storage and support tools. The team's privacy-oriented development experience provides a foundation for building and refining that integrated experience.

Longer-term defensibility will depend on the quality and consistency of the privacy capability, user trust and validated support relationships. These are advantages to develop and demonstrate, rather than claims of exclusive technology or existing partnerships.

## Customer Segments

Primary users: People in South Africa who need to document experiences involving gender-based violence, domestic abuse, harassment, intimidation, coercion or other personal safety concerns, regardless of gender. This includes men who may face stigma when seeking support or reporting abuse.

Early adoption communities: University students, young adults and digitally connected communities reached through accessible information and trusted referrals.

Secondary users: People seeking discreet personal documentation, sensitive journalling and greater control over personal information.

Potential institutional customers and partners: NGOs, victim-support organisations, educational institutions and community programmes that could support or sponsor access. These are prospective relationships, not established partnerships.

## Key Metrics

Primary measure: Whether LuLu helps an individual document their experience, access appropriate support and make progress towards an outcome they choose. Meaningful help for a small number of people matters more than a large registration count.

Impact indicators: Successful documentation and preservation of supporting material; continuity and retrieval of records when needed; voluntary feedback on confidence, comfort and control; successful connections to appropriate assistance; and user-reported progress towards personal goals. Goals may include privately preserving an account, seeking counselling or protection, or choosing to report.

During a controlled pilot, record the number and proportion of consenting participants who achieve their chosen documentation or support goals, alongside usability failures and privacy incidents. Use optional, minimal feedback without collecting incident narratives for analytics. Establish baselines before setting numerical targets; do not equate reporting or convictions with success for every user.

## Distribution Channels

Initial delivery: A mobile-first web application accessible through a browser, with native iOS and Android applications planned as the product develops.

Early awareness and adoption: South African university communities, student organisations, community networks, relevant digital communities and referrals from trusted individuals.

Partnership channels to explore: GBV and victim-support organisations, community NGOs, educational institutions, counselling networks and social-impact programmes. These relationships could support trusted introductions and sponsored access.

Hackathons, product demonstrations and privacy-awareness content can create initial visibility. Communications should respect discretion and avoid revealing someone's use of sensitive features. Channels and partnerships will be tested with potential users rather than assumed to be established.

## Cost Structure

Indicative incremental monthly budget for development and a small controlled pilot:

Hosting, authentication, database and server functions: R0–R300.
AI processing: R0–R500, initially supported by available API credits.
Evidence storage and data transfer: R0–R300.
Monitoring, testing and supporting services: R0–R200.
Additional domain and deployment expenses: R0–R100 where existing resources can be reused.

Total planning range: R0–R1,400 per month. These are estimates, not supplier quotes or measured operating costs. The lower end assumes existing resources, available allowances and credits; credits fund consumption rather than remove its underlying cost.

The estimate excludes development labour, hardware, legal/privacy review, professional services and maintaining support partnerships. Costs will be measured against active usage, processing volume and attachment storage, with budgets and usage alerts before scaling.

## Revenue Streams

Proposed sustainable-access model:

Free individual access to essential private documentation, protected journal entries, evidence organisation and support resources.

Optional premium subscription, indicatively R39–R59 per month, for expanded storage, enhanced narrative capabilities and additional organisation features. Pricing and willingness to pay require validation. For illustration only, 100 subscribers at R49 would generate R4,900 per month in gross subscription revenue, before costs, fees and taxes; this is not a forecast.

Institutional sponsorship and service agreements could fund access through NGOs, educational institutions and community organisations. Grants, foundations and corporate social-investment programmes are additional funding routes to explore.

Longer term, the privacy transformation capability may support business integrations or technology licensing. This is a separate future opportunity. The model does not rely on selling private narratives, evidence or identifying information.
